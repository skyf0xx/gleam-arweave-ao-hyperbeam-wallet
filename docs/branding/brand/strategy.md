# Gleam — Brand Strategy

---

## 01 — What is Gleam?

*"Simple. Fast. Easy."*

Gleam is the wallet for AO and Arweave that does five things — hold, receive, send, connect/sign, recover — and does them so plainly that using it never feels like a technical task. Simplicity is the whole pitch: instant balances, a clean asset view, effortless signing, nothing to configure and nothing to figure out. Everything underneath — scoped grants instead of open-ended connections, plain-language previews before you sign, a revoke that actually revokes, recovery you can test before you need it — exists for one reason: so that simplicity is never a lie. Keys and signatures are the mechanism, not the message. The message is that this wallet is just easy to use, every day, and stays that way because the mechanics underneath are actually sound.

Gleam refuses to become anything more than a wallet. No token, no DEX, no ecosystem dashboard, no app-builder layer — every one of those is a decision *not* to make, in service of staying the simplest, fastest, easiest thing in the category.

---

## 02 — Brand Vision

To become the wallet AO/Arweave defaults to — not by adding more, but by being the one that stays genuinely simple, fast, and easy while everything around it grows more complicated.

**Simple · Fast · Easy · Quiet**

---

## 03 — Brand Mission

Gleam exists to make holding and using AR and AO as simple as it should have always been — instant to open, obvious to use, and fast at every single step. The safety underneath (scoped permissions, visible previews, tested recovery) is what makes that simplicity real rather than superficial, but it is never the headline. The headline is always: this is the easy one.

**Simple · Fast · Easy · Sound**

## 04 — Brand Values

**Simple by design**
Every decision that doesn't need to exist gets cut — no manual network switching, no clutter, no settings maze. Simplicity is the product, not a coat of paint over a complex one.

**Fast, and it feels fast**
Balances load, buttons respond, nothing makes you wait and wonder if it worked. Speed is a daily, felt experience, not a claim on a features page.

**Easy from the first click**
Getting started and getting things done should never require cryptography homework. The easiest path is the default path, every time.

**Sound underneath — proof, not decoration**
The reason simple stays simple: safe-by-default signing, scoped permissions, a revoke that actually revokes, recovery you can test. None of this is the pitch — it's what keeps the pitch honest. No "bank-grade" language, no badges; if a claim can't be demonstrated, it isn't made.

**Small footprint, easy to audit**
Every dependency, every permission, every line of injected code is a liability Gleam tries to minimize rather than accumulate. Being small is not a phase before Gleam gets bigger — it's the point, and it's part of what keeps the wallet feeling simple rather than sprawling.

`Simple` · `Fast` · `Easy` · `Sound`

---

## 05 — Brand Goals

**Ship the five core jobs, exceptionally:** hold, receive, send, connect/sign, recover. Not a sixth feature — flawless, fast, obvious execution of the five things a wallet actually has to do.

**Make simplicity the headline, soundness the proof underneath:** users don't think in "grants" — they think in "is this easy and does it work." Simple/fast/easy is the entire pitch; capability-based, budgeted, expiring permissions with an absolute revoke are what make that simplicity trustworthy rather than shallow, surfaced only when someone looks for it.

**Prove recovery instead of hoping it works:** ship a "verify my recovery" dry-run that confirms a backup is valid without moving funds — simplicity has to hold up at the one moment it matters most.

**Win power users on reliability, not feature count:** named accounts, portfolio totals, flawless hardware wallet integration, and crash-safe state that survives a browser restart — proof that "easy" and "serious" aren't in tension.

**Stay disciplined as the category grows more complex:** no token, no DEX, no ecosystem dashboard, no app-builder layer. The wedge is narrowness; every feature considered gets measured against whether it keeps the wallet simple or quietly starts to erode it.

## 06 — Target Audience

People who already hold AR or AO and want a wallet that's simply easy to use — every day, without asking them to think about tokens, ecosystems, or configuration.

| | |
|---|---|
| Primary segment | AO/Arweave power users |
| Secondary segments | Active dApp/Bazar users, newcomers to AO/Arweave |
| Platform | Chrome extension (popup / side panel / full page) |
| Spending posture | Self-custody-first, fee-sensitive, distrustful of token-gated tiers |
| Focus | AO + Arweave only — no multichain breadth |

**Persona 1 — Dara, 34, Protocol Contributor, Remote / distributed team, self-employed**
*Has held AR since before AO existed; runs a hardware wallet for anything above a trivial balance.*
> "I want a wallet that's simple and just works — recovery that actually works, permissions that actually mean something when I revoke them."
- **Goals:** Sign AO messages confidently, keep a Ledger in the loop, know exactly what's connected to what.
- **Challenges:** Wants hardware wallet parity without extra setup steps; wants a single place to see what's connected, not permissions scattered across tabs.
- **Needs:** Hardware wallet parity from day one, a security screen that's actually one screen, a revoke that's actually absolute.
- **How Gleam solves it:** Ships hardware wallet support in v1, capability-based grants with absolute revoke, and one unified security screen — all in service of a wallet that stays simple even as Dara's usage gets more advanced.

**Persona 2 — Marcus, 27, active Bazar trader and dApp user, part-time freelance**
*Uses his wallet daily — Bazar trades, token transfers, connecting to new apps. High frequency of use means simplicity compounds: every bit of friction gets paid over and over.*
> "I'm in this thing multiple times a day. I just want to see what I have, send or approve something in two taps, and move on."
- **Goals:** See his total balance and everything he holds (tokens, NFTs) at a glance; send, receive, and approve transactions quickly, without the wallet slowing him down.
- **Challenges:** Clutter and extra steps cost him time every single day, not just once — any wallet that's slow or busy is a daily tax.
- **Needs:** A clean balance/holdings view, fast send/receive, and a trade-approval flow that doesn't make him stop and think each time.
- **How Gleam solves it:** A clean asset view with portfolio total up front, and send/receive/approve flows built to be fast by default — the scoped-grants/simulation machinery keeps him safe underneath, but what Marcus feels day to day is a wallet that never gets in his own way.

**Persona 3 — Priya, 41, small-business owner accepting AR and AO payments, browser/mobile only**
*Checks her wallet often to see what's come in from clients; no hardware wallet — a Ledger is an expense she has no reason to justify yet.*
> "I just want to open it, see what I have and what came in, and send or receive without thinking twice."
- **Goals:** See her total balance and holdings clearly, receive client payments reliably, send funds out easily when she needs to.
- **Challenges:** Seed-phrase-first onboarding intimidates her; as a frequent, non-technical user, any clutter or confusing step compounds every time she opens the wallet.
- **Needs:** A simple, honest balance and holdings view, and send/receive/approve that stays fast and obvious with daily use — all without needing to buy anything extra.
- **How Gleam solves it:** A clean asset view with an always-accurate balance, passkey-first setup with no seed/keyfile gate in the critical path, and send/receive/approve flows simple enough to stay effortless the hundredth time, not just the first.

## 07 — Brand Personality

**Axis summary:** Casual-leaning · Precise · Value-transparent · Mechanically confident · Quiet

**Personality Statement:** Gleam talks like the engineer who actually built the thing, not the marketer hired to describe it. It doesn't raise its voice to be believed — it shows you the mechanism and lets that be the argument. It has a dry, almost-playful edge in low-stakes moments and goes completely flat and literal the instant money or a signature is involved, because that's exactly when cleverness stops being welcome. It has opinions — no token, no tiers, no ecosystem sprawl — and states them as facts about what it refuses to build, not as a pitch.

---

## 08 — Brand Voice

*Gleam sounds like a precise, unshowy engineer who respects your attention — clear, quietly confident, and allergic to reassurance theater.*

Gleam's voice sits closer to casual than formal, calm rather than energetic, and accessible rather than authoritative-for-its-own-sake — but it never softens a claim it can't back up mechanically. It uses contractions, short sentences, and real numbers wherever a real number exists. It saves its only flashes of warmth or wit for moments that carry no financial or security weight, and turns completely plain the instant either does.

**Plain-spoken**
Say exactly what happens, in the order it happens. A signing request is a signing request, never an "authorization event."

**Mechanically confident, never asserted**
Trust is demonstrated — full addresses, visible grants, revoke that revokes — never claimed in adjectives like "bank-grade."

**Proportional**
Copy weight matches actual stakes: routine actions get a quiet confirmation, irreversible ones get a specific, unambiguous stop.

**Specific over hollow**
A number, a scope, a duration replaces a vague reassurance every time one is available — "expires in 24 hours," not "a safe, limited-time permission."

**Quietly humane**
Errors and recovery moments read like a competent person helping you, not a legal disclaimer or a cheerful bot.

**Small footprint**
No filler sentence before the point. If a sentence can be cut without losing meaning, it gets cut.

**Undistracted**
Never sells the ecosystem, the ambition, or the roadmap in-product. The product talks about what it's doing right now, not what it might become.

## 09 — Competitor Analysis

**Wander (formerly ArConnect)**
- **Overview:** The first and still-dominant AO/Arweave wallet extension.
- **Strengths:** First-mover distribution (~30,000+ Chrome users), broad dApp integration as the incumbent standard, mobile presence.
- **Weaknesses:** Feature accumulation (agents, a DEX, storage mining, a token with fee tiers) has made the core, everyday experience — checking a balance, sending, approving — feel cluttered and slower than it needs to be.
- **How Gleam is different:** Gleam is simple where Wander has grown complicated. The five core jobs get all the attention; nothing else competes for space in the interface.

**PermawebOS**
- **Overview:** A newer entrant positioning itself as a browser-based AO node with configurable routing policy and an in-wallet app-creation layer, not just a wallet.
- **Strengths:** Ambitious network-routing vision (user-controlled gateway/uploader/HyperBEAM endpoint choices), early Ledger support.
- **Weaknesses:** Built around platform ambition — routing policy, an app builder, ecosystem scope — stacked on top of a wallet experience that is, firsthand, harder to install and less pleasant to use day-to-day than it needs to be.
- **How Gleam is different:** Gleam bets that ease of use wins over ecosystem ambition. A wallet that's simply pleasant to install and use, every day, matters more than a routing-policy vision most people never touch.

**Phantom (cross-category reference, cited for a specific known flaw)**
- **Overview:** Not an AO/Arweave wallet, but a widely used multichain wallet referenced in the category for one specific, well-known trust failure.
- **Strengths:** Polished UX, broad multichain support, strong brand recognition.
- **Weaknesses:** Disconnecting an app does not revoke its underlying token/spend approvals — users assume disconnect means revoked, and it doesn't.
- **How Gleam is different:** Gleam's revoke model is built specifically to never reproduce this — disconnecting an app in Gleam ends every bit of access it had, full stop, by design rather than by patch.

## 10 — Positioning

**Positioning Narrative:** AO/Arweave wallets keep adding scope — tokens, DEXes, routing dashboards, app-builder layers — in pursuit of ecosystem relevance, and the everyday experience of just holding and using AR/AO gets more cluttered with each addition. Nobody has stayed simple on purpose. Gleam's whole bet is that simple, fast, and easy is the actual differentiator in this category — not a stepping stone to a bigger platform, not a "for beginners" tier, but the permanent, disciplined position. Everything Gleam builds underneath (scoped grants, visible previews, tested recovery) exists to keep that simplicity honest, not to compete with it for attention.

**Positioning Statement:** Gleam is the simple, fast, easy wallet for AO and Arweave — the one that stays a wallet, and nothing more, so that using it never feels like a technical task.

**Positioning Map:** Two axes — *Scope* (single-purpose wallet ←→ ecosystem platform) and *Usability* (clunky/hard-to-use ←→ simple/easy-to-use). Other wallets in the category trend toward the platform end of the scope axis as they mature, often at the cost of usability. Gleam sits alone in the single-purpose / high-usability quadrant — the only one actively refusing to trade ease-of-use for feature scope.

---

## 11 — How Gleam Stands Out

*Simple. Fast. Easy.*

Gleam refuses the instinct that shapes most of this category over time: that a wallet should grow into a platform. It will not add a token, a DEX, an app builder, or a routing-policy dashboard in pursuit of ecosystem relevance. What it commits to instead is simplicity that actually holds up — a wallet that just works, the way a car that drives isn't a feature list, it's the baseline you shouldn't have to think about. Recovery working, revoke meaning revoke, a balance you can trust — none of that is the pitch. It's what "simple" has to include to not be a lie.

Every other wallet in this category treats depth and reliability as things you earn by adding more. Gleam's bet is the opposite: staying narrow is what makes simple, fast, and easy actually true, every day, rather than a claim that stops holding up the moment you look closely.

## 12 — Tagline Suggestions

**"Simple. Fast. Easy."**
Three flat, independently provable claims (load time, click-to-response, first-run flow) that double as a literal checklist reviewers can hold the product against — the mantra, and the team's selected direction in messaging.md.

**"See what you're signing."**
States one proof point behind the simplicity claim — visible transaction consequence — as a plain, checkable fact. A strong secondary line for security/signing-specific contexts, not the headline.

**"Grants, not connections."**
Names one specific mechanical proof point (capability-based permissions) sharply — best used as a supporting-message headline in technical/docs contexts, not the general brand line.

**"The wallet that isn't a chore."**
A direct, witty way of stating the simplicity claim against the category's general complexity — works without naming any competitor.

Recommendation stands with the team's own selection, **"Simple. Fast. Easy."**, as the mantra and mission in one line — with **"See what you're signing."** as a security-context secondary line, both already reflected in messaging.md's hierarchy.
