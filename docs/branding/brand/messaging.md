# Gleam: Messaging Framework

## 01: Core Message

Gleam makes crypto feel simple, clear, and effortless — a wallet you barely have to think about.

## 02: Value Proposition

Gleam is your go-to wallet for AO and Arweave. It removes the friction and complexity that tends to build up around crypto wallets, so opening it, checking a balance, sending something, or signing never feels like a technical task. No clutter, no unnecessary ceremony, no guessing what just happened — just the five things a wallet has to do, done so plainly you stop noticing you're using software at all.

## 03: Tagline Options

**"Simple. Fast. Easy."**
- Style: functional
- Rationale: Three flat claims, no qualifiers. This is the selected direction and the anchor for everything below.

**"Just a wallet."**
- Style: functional
- Rationale: The flattest possible statement of the position — a refusal, not a pitch. Strong as a secondary line or a section header.

**"The wallet that isn't a chore."**
- Style: witty
- Rationale: States the feeling Gleam avoids without describing anyone else. Works for anyone who's found wallets tedious, without needing to have a specific one in mind.

**"One thing. Done well."**
- Style: functional
- Rationale: Plain restatement of the scope — works well as a footer line or an about-page headline, less distinctive as a primary tagline on its own.

**"A clear light on AO."**
- Style: aspirational
- Rationale: Plays on the name itself (gleam, a beam of clean light) and the AO/Arweave-only focus, but trades functional clarity for wordplay.

**Recommended: "Simple. Fast. Easy."** This is the selected tagline. It's three flat, provable claims instead of one abstract promise. Each word is independently demonstrable (load time, click-to-response, first-run flow). It works as a tagline, a three-bullet proof structure, and a literal checklist reviewers can hold the product against.

## 04: Messaging Hierarchy

**Level 1: Brand Headline:**
Simple. Fast. Easy.

**Level 2: Supporting Statement:**
Gleam is your go-to wallet for AO and Arweave: instant balances, clear transactions, effortless signing. Gleam is what a wallet looks like when it's built to work.

**Level 3: Key Messages**

**1. Simple by design**
Gleam cuts every decision that doesn't need to exist: no manual network switching, no clutter, no settings maze. What's left is a wallet that's genuinely easy to use, the kind of ease that survives contact with the real product.
- Proof point: One network (AO/Arweave), one clean asset view, one screen for everything connected.

**2. Fast, and it feels fast**
Balances load. Buttons respond. The interface never makes you wait and wonder if it's working. Speed here isn't a marketing adjective. It's the thing you notice in the first ten seconds of using it.
- Proof point: The wallet is always in a working state — it opens the same way every time, whether it's the first use or the thousandth.

**3. Easy from the first click**
Setup skips anything that feels like homework. You're using it within a minute of installing it, not reading a security tutorial first.
- Proof point: Install, set up, wallet ready, guided first action — no cryptography lesson required to get started.

**4. Nothing else to learn or manage**
There's no token to track, no DEX to understand, no dashboard of settings you didn't ask for. Gleam is the same five things, done well, every time you open it.
- Proof point: No token, no DEX, no ecosystem tab, no app-builder — the interface only ever shows what a wallet needs to show.

**Level 4: Proof Point Bank**
- Simple. Fast. Easy. Three provable claims, each independently demonstrable: load time, click-to-response, and a first-run flow that needs no explanation.
- One network, AO and Arweave only — no network switcher, no multichain settings to manage.
- One clean asset view and one place for everything connected — nothing scattered across tabs.
- Setup that gets you to your first action fast, without a security tutorial first.
- A named, guided path for anyone moving over from another wallet.
- No token, no DEX, no ecosystem dashboard, no app-builder layer — nothing competing for your attention that isn't the wallet itself.
- AO and Arweave only. One clear focus, nothing to switch between.

## 05: Audience-Specific Messaging

**For users migrating an existing wallet:**
- What they care most about: Whether the move is easy, safe, and quick — not losing anything in the process.
- How to lead the message: Lead with simple/fast/easy as Gleam's own standard, and show that moving over takes minutes and is fully guided.
- Proof points that land hardest: A named, guided migration path, a clean asset view from the first screen, nothing extra to configure after moving over.
- Avoid: Framing the migration around what's wrong with where they're coming from — the message is what Gleam offers, not a critique of the wallet they're leaving.

**For AO/Arweave power users (multi-account, hardware wallet users):**
- What they care most about: Speed, account organization, hardware wallet reliability, and being taken seriously.
- How to lead the message: Lead with "simple" as an observable fact, not a beginner's feature. Simple and serious aren't in tension here — show it through how fast and unclutterred the experience stays even with real usage.
- Proof points that land hardest: Named accounts with one-click switching, portfolio total across accounts, flawless hardware wallet integration, a wallet that stays out of your way while it works.
- Avoid: Talking down to this audience by over-explaining basics, or implying "simple" means "limited."

**For newcomers to AO/Arweave (no prior wallet):**
- What they care most about: Getting it right, keeping their funds safe, getting started without needing to understand cryptography first.
- How to lead the message: Lead with "easy" — getting set up and taking a first action without a security tutorial standing in the way.
- Proof points that land hardest: A setup that gets out of the way fast, a guided first action, nothing extra to learn before you can use it.
- Avoid: Front-loading mechanism or security language before they've done anything — that's not the pitch, and it's not what gets them started.

## 07: Things Not to Say

- No trust-by-assertion claims: "bank-grade," "military-grade," "industry-leading security" — trust isn't the pitch here at all; simplicity is.
- No multichain or feature-count framing ("supports X chains") — the AO/Arweave-only focus is a positioning choice, not a limitation to apologize for.
- No generic wallet clichés: "your gateway to web3," "unlock the power of decentralization."
- Don't call the migration flow "import a wallet" in any Wander-facing message — it's a named, specific migration path.
- Don't itemize or headline the underlying safety mechanics (recovery, permissions, previews) as marketed features or a proof-point checklist — they're incidental outcomes of a well-made simple wallet, not capabilities being sold. If it starts to read as "look how much this does," cut it.
- Stay competitor-silent to outsiders, fully — no naming a competitor, and no unnamed digs either ("what others get wrong," "unlike other wallets," "the bloat everyone else has"). Public messaging states what Gleam is and does; it never defines itself against another product, even implicitly. Competitor analysis stays internal (strategy.md, positioning.md) and informs decisions, not outward copy.
- Don't let "simple" read as "basic" — every simplicity claim should imply removed friction, not removed capability.
- Don't claim "open source" or "audit" as a headline trust badge — it's not the pitch; if it comes up at all, it's a quiet, secondary fact for anyone who goes looking.
