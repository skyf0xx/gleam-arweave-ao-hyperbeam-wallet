# Brand Positioning — Gleam

## 01 — Category Definition

**Stated category**: Crypto/web3 wallet — browser extension for Arweave and AO.

**Actual category**: When an AO/Arweave user picks a wallet today, they're comparing it against "ecosystem platforms that happen to include a wallet" — Wander (agents, DEX, storage mining, $WNDR token) and PermawebOS (browser-node, routing policy, an app-creation layer). The comparison set has drifted from "wallet" to "permaweb access platform," and neither incumbent is contesting that drift — they're both racing further into it.

**Opportunity category**: Gleam refuses the platform category and stays a **wallet** — nothing more, said plainly, with no hedge. Trust is table stakes in this category (it's blockchain — self-custody already implies a trust claim every product makes); it's not a lever anyone actually feels or compares on. What people do notice, immediately and without being told to look for it, is whether a piece of software is simple or not. That's the axis nobody in this category is willing to compete on, because winning it means refusing feature after feature that would be easy to ship.

**Recommendation**: Frame Gleam as being in the **"just a wallet"** category, not "the safest wallet" or "the most complete wallet." The comparison question Gleam wants users asking is "why does this feel so much lighter than the others?" — not "why should I trust this?" (a question every wallet already answers "yes" to, credibly or not) and not "what else can this do?" (a question Gleam actively refuses).

## 02 — Competitive Landscape Mapping

**Axis 1**: Narrow/Wallet-only ← → Broad/Platform
**Axis 2**: Complicated to use ← → Simple to use

- **Wander**: Broad/Platform, Complicated. Agents, a DEX, storage mining, a token with fee tiers, autonomous yield — all stacked onto the core wallet screen. Even the basics (balance, send, connect) are reported as unreliable, and the platform sprawl is the reason: attention and surface area are split across ten things instead of concentrated on five.
- **PermawebOS**: Even broader/Platform, more Complicated still. Routing policy, node concepts, an app-creation layer (Ouroboros) — a product built around what it does technically, not around how it feels to use. Installation itself is a pain. It reads as infrastructure wearing a wallet's face.
- **MetaMask** (reference point): Broad/Platform, Complicated by accumulation — the default multichain-wallet shape this whole category inherited features from.
- **Rabby** (reference point): Narrower than MetaMask, moderately simple — cleaner UX than most EVM wallets, but still multichain-broad in scope.
- **Beacon**: Narrow/Wallet-only, moderately simple — mobile-signing keeps the core flow tight, but multisig/shared-wallet features add scope back in.
- **aoWebWallet**: Narrow/Wallet-only, utilitarian rather than designed for simplicity — a lightweight tool, not a brand built around the feeling of ease.

**Where Gleam sits**: Narrow/Wallet-only, maximally Simple. That quadrant is **unclaimed**. Every other AO/Arweave wallet either goes broad (Wander, PermawebOS) or stays narrow without making simplicity itself the product experience (Beacon, aoWebWallet). Nobody has planted a flag on "does one thing, does it with nothing in the way."

## 03 — Positioning Territory

**The Space**: The feeling of a tool that gets out of your way — you open it, you do the thing, you close it, and nothing about that experience made you feel like you were managing software.

**The Audience Owned**: AO/Arweave users — including Wander refugees drowning in a platform they didn't ask for — who want a wallet, not an ecosystem, not a dashboard, not a second job.

**The Competitive Moat**: Both incumbents are structurally committed to breadth — Wander's roadmap and revenue model (the $WNDR token, agents, DEX) depend on platform expansion, and PermawebOS inherits the identical instinct from the same team. Neither can retreat to simple without abandoning their own strategy, their monetization, and the sunk cost of everything they've already shipped. Gleam's simplicity isn't a launch-stage limitation to grow out of — it's the whole point, which makes it a durable position rather than a head start a competitor copies next quarter.

---

## 04 — Positioning Statement

**Primary**: For AO/Arweave users tired of wallets that turned into platforms, Gleam is the wallet that does one thing — being a wallet — and does it without asking you to learn, configure, or manage anything else.

**Strategic (internal)**: Gleam competes in AO/Arweave wallets by refusing the platform-expansion strategy both incumbents are structurally locked into, and instead concentrating entirely on the five jobs a wallet has to do — hold, receive, send, sign, recover — with everything about the experience engineered to feel effortless rather than capable.

**Human (public-facing)**: Gleam's just a wallet. On purpose. Open it, do the thing, done.

---

## 05 — Positioning Proof Points

Simplicity isn't a feature list — it's the absence of one. The things that make Gleam feel simple aren't front-loaded as selling points; they're just what happens, quietly, when you need them:

- **No seed-phrase ritual to learn.** Onboarding doesn't hand you homework before you can use the thing.
- **One plain-language screen when an app asks for access** — not a set of permission enums to decode.
- **You always know what happens next** — no surprise outcomes, no second-guessing after the fact.
- **Nothing to configure to feel safe.** Recovery, revocation, and previewing consequences aren't settings you have to find and turn on — they're just how the wallet behaves.
- **No token, no DEX, no agents, no app-builder.** Nothing extra competing for a screen, a decision, or your attention.

None of these get marketed as capabilities. They surface at the moment they're needed and disappear again — that's what "simple" means in practice, and it's the whole differentiation.

---

## 06 — What This Brand Refuses to Be

- Gleam is not for people who want their wallet to also be a DEX, an agent runtime, or a storage marketplace — go use Wander for that.
- Gleam will never compete on feature count, ecosystem breadth, or chain count.
- Gleam does not try to be an infrastructure platform, a routing-policy tool, or an app-creation layer — that's PermawebOS's bet, not this one.
- Gleam does not sell safety or trust as a headline — those are just consequences of being simple, not the pitch itself.

---

## 07 — Positioning in One Sentence

Gleam is the AO/Arweave wallet that does one thing — being a wallet — better than anything that's trying to do ten.

---

## Red Flags Checked

- Not genericizable to competitors — "simple" is a real, structural refusal for Gleam (no token, no roadmap breadth) in a way it structurally cannot be for Wander or PermawebOS.
- Audience is bounded (AO/Arweave users, explicitly including a named Wander-migration segment) — not "everyone who wants a good wallet."
- Watch: "simple" is a claim every product makes. The differentiator that survives scrutiny is refusal — the list of things Gleam will never ship — not an adjective. Keep messaging anchored to what's absent, not to reassurance language ("easy," "effortless") that reads as unsubstantiated on its own.
- Trust/safety mechanics (recovery, grants, preview) are real and still shipping, but deliberately kept out of the headline positioning and out of proof-point marketing — they're incidental outcomes of simplicity, not things to itemize or lead with.
